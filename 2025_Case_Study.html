01 The Challenge

How do you fly where there is no GPS?

IRoC-U 2025 asked students to develop Autonomous Navigation for an Aerial Vehicle (ANAV) for Martian-surface conditions, without GNSS or any external navigation aid. Over 500 participants entered, and only 16 teams reached the finals.

01

Navigate

Fly autonomously across a Mars-like surface with no GNSS, pseudolites or reflector arrays to lean on.

02

Analyse terrain

Scan and understand the ground below in order to decide where it is safe to go.

03

Land & return

Detect a safe landing area, touch down, and complete the return part of the mission.

02 Our Perspective

On Mars, the vehicle has to be its own guide.

With no satellite signal to rely on, every decision to fly, look, land and return had to come from the vehicle itself.

03 Our Approach

One vehicle. Fully self-reliant.

We keep the focus on the bigger picture: a vehicle that can fly, see, decide and return on its own. Detailed design choices stay internal, so this page covers the public view only.

01

Fly

Controlled autonomous flight without external navigation aids.

02

Scan

Real-time terrain scanning as the vehicle moves.

03

Spot

Identify where a safe landing is possible.

04

Decide

Turn what the vehicle senses into actions, onboard and on its own.

05

Return

Support the return leg and the next stage of the mission.

06

Stay independent

Keep the system self-reliant, because on Mars nobody can send a signal to help.

From a proposal to the Top 16.

The 2025 challenge ran through qualification, a live-evaluated elimination round and a final field round at URSC, Bengaluru, ending with the awards on National Space Day.

05 What Stood Out

Two moments that defined the year.

Elimination Round

Proven live, on camera

The vehicle was evaluated in real time over live video calls, with an ISRO scientist on site. That performance earned a place among the top 16 finalists.

National Space Day

Felicitated at Bharat Mandapam

On 23 August 2025, the team was felicitated by Dr. Jitendra Singh, Minister of State, Department of Space, and Dr. V. Narayanan, Chairman ISRO.
